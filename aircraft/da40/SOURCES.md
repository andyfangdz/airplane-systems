# DA40 XLS systems research and applicability

Research inspected October 12, 2026. The modeled configuration is the **avgas DA40 XLS**, Lycoming **IO-360-M1A**, non-feathering three-blade **MTV-12-B/183-59b** propeller, Power Flow exhaust, long-range tanks, legacy **G1000 with WAAS and GFC 700**. The aircraft geometry remains the existing N949KC/XLS reference. This is not the diesel DA40 TDI/NG, the carbureted DA40F, LASAR ignition, G1000 NXi or KAP 140 installation.

The model is a schematic study aid. In X-ray the enclosing engine case/sump, cylinder barrels/heads/fins and rocker covers are ghosted as cutaways to expose internal hardware; solid mode preserves their normal enclosed appearance. Undimensioned part shapes, brackets, sensors, tube bores and installation bends are approximate; the sources establish component roles and system topology, not surveyed installation coordinates. Animated pressure and sensor particles illustrate connections, not continuous pneumatic airflow or measured data throughput.

## Coverage and decisions

- **Engine and ignition:** four staggered cylinders, crankshaft/camshaft, pistons/rods, push-rod shrouds, rockers/valves, eight spark plugs and high-tension harnesses, independent magnetos/P-leads, SlickSTART and starter. AMM 74-00-00 pp. 6–7 establishes upper-left/lower-right from the left magneto, upper-right/lower-left from the right, and both live during START. No unverified timing or harness part number is asserted. Individual magneto failures affect the selected ignition path and RPM response; electrical bus loss does not stop healthy running magnetos.
- **Induction and metering:** the paper filter attaches through a flexible-sealed lower-cowl housing to the fuel injector. Alternate air rotates a six-port sleeve/flange, manually by Bowden cable. Servo inlet screen, air/fuel diaphragms, mixture/idle metering and the divider diaphragm/needle are distinct from electronic indication sensors. A blocked primary intake can be recovered with alternate air in the study simulation. Filtered/alternate induction and all four manifold paths share the physical turning gate during cranking and windmilling; hot-exhaust particles require combustion.
- **Oil and propeller:** sump screen → accessory pump → thermostatic cooler/bypass → full-flow filter/bypass → pressure regulation/galleries → sump, with head supply/drains. Governor pump, flyweights, spring/pilot valve, crankshaft oil delivery and hub piston/return spring represent the non-feathering oil-to-coarse installation. The animation’s 150°F crossover, oil/RPM transient response and particle rates are illustrative, not maintenance values.
- **Fuel:** three connected aluminum chambers per side for long-range tanks; large flexible liquid couplings and smaller upper vent/balance hoses; independent capillary/check vents; inboard and long-range capacitive probes. Feed runs from the inboard finger filter through the below-floor selector, gascolator, electric pump/bypass and firesleeved firewall fitting. For this Lycoming AFM chain the **flow transducer is before the servo**, followed by divider/four nozzles. No Continental circulation/return or diesel transfer circuit is added. Cranking and windmilling drive the mechanical pump; boost priming, cold-start fuel availability and mixture cut-off use the same supply boundary throughout the routes. Selected-tank fuel alone cannot start an engine if both delivery paths are unavailable.
- **Electrical and avionics:** preserves existing aircraft-specific essential/main/avionic distribution and GFC 700 interaction logic. Added relays, one-way bypass diode, bus bars, LRU power/data routes, GIA/AHRS lightning blocks, shield/ground blocks and GEA backshell/configuration hardware. GDC/GRS/GIA/display paths, engine/quantity inputs, servo power/data/enable and lamp wiring remain distinct. The GFC 700 has three pitch/roll/trim servos and no yaw servo. Each GIA servo-data link requires its own powered source and the powered AFCS receiver; losing the other GIA leaves that physical link and protected servo power live while overall AP/MET availability is lost (Garmin SMM Figure 2-1, p. 2-7; AFMS 7-5).
- **Flight controls/flaps:** preserves the existing detailed moving control rig, and adds actual five-switch actuator roles, servo-mount/slip-clutch detail, pedal-adjustment motor and mechanical engine/propeller Bowden controls. The flap actuator is under the **left** rear passenger seat (AMM 27-50), not the right.
- **Pitot/static/stall:** common LH heated mast feeds GDC and independent pneumatic standby branches; airspeed also has its previously missing static branch. Below-pilot-seat water traps are **parallel lower sump loops between tees**, with a direct upper pressure run, not a reservoir in series. Mast and alternate-cabin branches join a common static tee with a separate pressure line to GDC and both standby instruments, independent of ADC power. Thermal switch is on the probe and fuse built in. The red-ring leading-edge stall inlet/hose/horn is pneumatic and remains independent of power.
- **Gear/cabin/environment/lighting:** main wheel bearings, caliper/piston/bleed fittings, both reservoirs on copilot masters, separate copilot → pilot → parking-valve → caliper circuits, parking valve linkage; separate canopy/rear-door bolts and lock inputs; documented RH intake adaptor/alternator cooling, shroud/heat dump, floor/defrost distribution, LH stub-wing collector/cross pipe/roll-bar feed and inverter/dimmer. OAM 40-099 adds **independent** LH/RH front-fuselage NACA panel-vent hoses; the Environment control can remove that option’s ports/nozzles/routes. Narrow wing-leading-edge and cowling inspection meshes are shown only during their spotlight, and existing real stall-strip/wingtip geometry is used.

## Applicability disagreements and uncertainty

1. The generic AMM 28-10 chamber-capacity paragraph sums to about 25 US gal, whereas the selected AFM 7-36/37 states **25 usable, 25.5 total per side**. The model keeps the AFM 50 usable / 51 total and established G1000 plateau behavior; individual chamber shapes do not assert exact volumes.
2. AMM 32 text reverses the parking-brake lever directions printed in AFM 7-13. The modeled **DOWN = set, UP = release** follows the AFM. The documented pedal-pumping procedure is retained.
3. Generic AMM 28-40 describes older display-processing/legacy quantity instruments and an AUX quantity switch. The selected G1000 uses its AFM/GEA calibration and quantity behavior; the obsolete interface is excluded.
4. AMM 34-10 describes rear autopilot static ports under OAM 40-267 for a different optional installation. The selected GFC 700 uses GDC air data from the common mast; no KAP 140 rear-static plumbing is assumed.
5. The base AFM and generic AMM 61 drawing describe older MTV-12-B/180-17 variants. The actually inspected approved STC SA06-52 extract establishes the selected 183-59b configuration and removes the older 2,400 RPM continuous limit. General non-feathering governor principles apply; no obsolete RPM restriction is copied.
6. Power Flow 41301/41303 serial applicability is documented; N949KC’s exhaust serial is unconfirmed. The model explicitly uses a schematic later 41303 collector and excludes the optional early header preheat sleeve. Collector/shroud shape and hose dimensions remain approximate.
7. Sensor component identities and signal destinations are supported by Garmin SMM; stations without surveyed drawings are approximate. A low indicated fuel pressure is not universally diagnosed as an actual pump failure. The simulation models actual pump failures; it does not invent a new approved troubleshooting procedure.

## Type-club context

The [Diamond Aviators “Low fuel pressure... again” discussion, page5](https://diamondaviators.net/forum/viewtopic.php?start=40&t=3671), posts January–July 2012, was actually inspected. Members discuss a proposed pressure-sender fault, electric-pump replacement, vapor/flow-transducer effects and later unresolved zero-pressure indication unchanged by boost. One post reproduces a purported Diamond reply secondhand. This supports showing **indication sensors separately from physical feed**, while the primary AFM/AMM controls capacities, plumbing and procedures. It is not used to create a new operating limit, a universal diagnosis, or an undocumented vapor-return line.

## Physical checks

The added and corrected fixed hardware, closed-door hardware and actual rendered curved tube walls are tested against the model’s fuselage/wing/stabilizer/fin skin. Moving-group geometry is also covered by the fleet test, including triangle centroids and edge midpoints. The undimensioned elevator horn uses a compact arm inside the root skin and its linkage follows the same attachment, avoiding a face across the simplified fin/stabilizer skin gap. `airframe-ports.ts` permits only short named skin crossings at physical vent/probe/lamp/overboard/horn/root-fairing interfaces; interior portions remain checked. Real exposed horns, mast, gear fittings, windows/handles and inspection surface overlays are explicitly classified as exterior. These are model-consistency checks, not certification of real-aircraft clearances.

## Cabin routing verification

Long electrical/data connections and the fresh-air cross-fuselage duct clear the modeled seats through underfloor/sidewall lanes. The parallel pressure-trap tees sit below the cushions; their low-point sumps, filters and instrument topology remain distinct. Tee heights and bend coordinates are schematic, not surveyed installation dimensions. Complete tube vertices, triangle centers and edge midpoints are checked against occupied cabin space and actual seat meshes, alongside the existing skin-containment checks.

## Working openings and mechanisms

AFM 6.01.01-E 7-13/14 describes the brake pedals and four masters, with pilot/copilot masters in series for each wheel; 7-17/18 describes canopy/rear-door locks and the rear-door gas-pressure damper. The rear strut now has a fixed-length barrel and sliding rod connected to the actual rendered door pose. Toe pressure moves the applicable piston rods and pedals; retained parking pressure does not hold the pedals depressed. The canopy bolts retract in OPEN, remain engaged in the cooling gap, and the rear locks/lever follow their control.

The shared curved-skin triangulation, panel parenting and strut helpers use the same source catalogue and
lossless prebuilt pipeline as the SR22T. Opening angles, hinge/anchor offsets, clearances, hardware shapes and
mechanism travel are illustrative, not manufacturer rigging dimensions. Existing station profiles and traced
outlines are retained. Functional/surface checks and remaining fleet gaps are listed in [MODELING.md](../../MODELING.md).
